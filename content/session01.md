# Session 01: Human Learning, Perception, and Creativity

<img src="../assets/session01_01.png" alt="Session 01 illustration" width="100%">

**Central question:** What makes human learning and creativity different from what AI does, and why does that matter?

**140 minutes, including a 10-minute break · Mainly pairs and short class discussions**

Work with the same partner for most activities, then join another pair for one small-group discussion. Keep short notes on paper or your device. Every activity tells you what to **do** and what to **produce**. These are thinking aids, **not graded submissions**. No advance reading, slides, or special equipment required.

## 1. How do we learn? (50 min)

### Cat recognition (10 min)

Imagine teaching a computer to distinguish a **real cat** from a **plush cat**.

|                                                                      Real cat                                                                      |                                                             Plush cat                                                             |
| :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------: | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------: |
| <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Tabby_cat_on_a_chair_%28Unsplash%29.jpg?width=480" alt="Real tabby cat" width="220"> | <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/TCMI_Cheshire_Cat_2.jpg?width=480" alt="Plush cat toy" width="220"> |

**Do (pairs):** Invent two rules for recognizing real cats. Find a possible image that would break at least one rule. Improve that rule.

**Produce:** **Two rules, one counterexample, and one improvement.**

### Machine learning and the black box (15 min)

Follow a short introduction using the [AI Learning slides](../slides/session01.html). In programming, we specify rules; in machine learning, we train a model using data and feedback. A trained model can make a new prediction without necessarily learning from that particular prediction. Its output does not always explain how it arrived at the result.

**Do (pairs):** Return to your cat rules. Compare rewriting a rule with training on more examples. Discuss whether a deployed model automatically learns whenever it makes a mistake.

**Produce:** Complete these two sentences:

- "A programmed rule changes when..., while a trained model improves when..."
- "From the prediction alone, we cannot know..."

### Human learning: Charles Pépin (15 min)

**Open the [Human Learning guide](./human-learning.md#why-failure-matters)** and read the short explanation of Pépin's idea that human dependence and failure can create opportunities for learning, culture, and freedom.

**Do (pairs):**

1. Recall something you learned through mistakes. What actually helped you improve?
2. Open the [Human Learning NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/f147adf8-e289-4bc4-938e-350a61e5a484) and ask:

   > Why does Charles Pépin connect failure with learning, culture, and inventing ourselves? Give one example from the sources.

3. Compare the answer with your experience. You may disagree with Pépin. If NotebookLM isn't available, use the [short transcript](../slides/static/vids/philo_en.md#learning-through-culture-and-failure).

**Produce:** **One personal example** and **one sentence** explaining whether it supports or challenges Pépin's argument.

### Discussion: What does it mean to learn? (10 min)

**Do:** Prepare one answer to **"Is making a mistake enough to learn, for a person or a machine?"** Listen to different responses during the class discussion.

**Produce:** **One position and one reason**, ready to share if invited. No pair presentations.

**Break (10 min)**

## 2. Perception, the body, and creativity (40 min)

### Berkeley: If the Sensors Go Dark (10 min)

![A robot's sensors go dark](../assets/a_sensor_goes_dark.png)

Imagine a robot with a camera and a saved map of a room. The camera is disconnected, but its map remains.

**Open the [Berkeley section of the Perception guide](./perception-guide.md#berkeley-a-map-isnt-a-world).** Berkeley asks us to question the relation between perception and existence; he does not simply claim that objects vanish when one person stops looking.

**Do (pairs):** Does the robot's world still exist? Distinguish the **physical room**, the **stored map**, and what the robot can **currently sense**. Is a map the same as experiencing a room?

**Produce:** **Two short sentences** giving your answer and the distinction behind it. Add an unanswered question if you have one.

### Touch recognition and Merleau-Ponty (20 min)

![Embodied or disembodied perception](../assets/embodied_or_disembodied.png)

**Do (pairs):**

1. **Ask AI (3 min):** Ask any available LLM:

   > Describe how someone recognizes a familiar object using only touch. Include hand movements and clues, in fewer than 100 words.

2. **Try it (7 min):** Use safe everyday objects you already have, such as a pen or keyring. One partner explores an object by touch, without seeing it, and tries to identify it. Swap roles. Touch only objects you're comfortable with.
3. **Compare (5 min):** Which movements or sensations helped you? What did the AI capture or miss?
4. **Connect (5 min):** Read [Merleau-Ponty: perceiving by doing](./perception-guide.md#merleau-ponty-perceiving-by-doing). How did movement help you understand the object?

**Produce:** **Two observations from the experiment and one comparison** with the AI's description.

_Alternative:_ If touching an object isn't suitable, discuss how a robot would recognize and pick it up with and without the ability to move. Note one insight.

### Discussion: What does the body contribute to creativity? (10 min)

**Do:** Think of drawing, music, prototyping, editing, or another practice. What do you discover by **making** something rather than planning it entirely in advance?

**Produce:** **One concrete example:** "When I make..., I discover... by doing..." Share it if invited.

## 3. Creativity and society: a literary controversy (25 min)

**Open [Creativity and Society](./creativity-and-society.md) now. This is your working guide for all four activities below.** It contains the case facts, the used-car analogy, and stakeholder perspectives. You do **not** need to listen to the French podcast.

### The Orélien case (5 min)

**Do (pairs):** Read the guide's [case file](./creativity-and-society.md#case-file-a-literary-controversy) (2 min), then discuss (3 min): imagine you loved a novel and later heard an allegation that AI helped write it. Would it change your judgment? What would you want to know?

The allegations about this specific author are disputed. **Do not try to decide whether they are true.**

**Produce:** **One sentence** explaining your position and why.

### Trust and the "lemons" analogy (5 min)

**Do (pairs):** Read [Used cars and trust](./creativity-and-society.md#used-cars-and-trust-the-lemons-analogy) (2 min). Then discuss (3 min): if readers cannot easily know how a book was made, how could uncertainty affect trust, including in authors who never used AI?

**Produce:** Complete: **"Just as car buyers cannot easily know..., readers cannot easily know..., which could affect..."**

_The analogy concerns missing information and trust, not a claim that AI-assisted books are inferior._

### Stakeholder investigation (8 min)

**Do (groups of four):** Join another pair. Use the guide's [stakeholder table and six questions](./creativity-and-society.md#stakeholders-six-questions-one-controversy). Pick two perspectives (for example, writer and reader). Compare what each might value, gain, or risk, then find one disagreement.

**Produce:** **Three short lines:** the first perspective, the second perspective, and their main disagreement.

### Discussion: What do we value in creation? (7 min)

**Do:** Choose one spokesperson per group to share the disagreement if invited. Use the [connections to learning and perception](./creativity-and-society.md#connecting-learning-perception-and-creativity) to think back to Pépin and Merleau-Ponty.

**Question:** Is a creative work's value only in the result, or also in human effort, experience, intention, and relationships?

**Produce:** **One disagreement to share and one question left open.**

## 4. A starting point for Assignment 1 (15 min)

This is **inspiration**, not a project proposal or an additional assessment. Follow the official assignment brief for requirements and deadlines.

**Do:**

1. **Individual (5 min):** Choose an AI use case that interests you and a group of people it affects.
2. **Pairs (5 min):** Explain your question to your partner. Check whether the situation and stakes are clear, then refine it.
3. **Closing (5 min):** We'll hear a few possible questions, not project presentations.

**Produce:** A **three-line starting point**, for you to keep:

- **AI use case:** ...
- **People affected and what changes for them:** ...
- **Question I might explore:** ...

**For further ideation (optional):** Return to the [Human Learning NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/f147adf8-e289-4bc4-938e-350a61e5a484) or [Perception NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/af5ed21e-57cd-4d0a-b2a1-8c96e3e75d74). Ask for _questions inspired by sources_, not finished projects. The [France Culture debate](https://www.radiofrance.fr/franceculture/podcasts/questions-du-soir-le-debat/affaire-thelyson-orelien-avant-de-tous-nous-tuer-l-ia-va-t-elle-d-abord-nous-diviser-7201314) is separate optional listening.
