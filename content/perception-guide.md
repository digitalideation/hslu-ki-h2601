# Perception: From Data to Experience

**Use this guide in class during [Part 2: Perception, the body, and creativity](./session01.md#2-perception-the-body-and-creativity-40-min).** Read **Berkeley** for the robot question, and **Merleau-Ponty** after the touch experiment. The further resources below are optional.

**Question:** What is the difference between recognizing something and experiencing it, and how might bodily experience shape creativity?

All instructions and durations are on the [classroom page](./session01.md), while this guide supplies the philosophical ideas needed to interpret those activities. No advance reading required.

## Berkeley: a map isn't a world

Imagine the robot's camera has been disconnected but its stored map remains. Distinguish the physical room, the map in memory, and the robot's current access to the room. Are these three things equivalent?

Berkeley questioned the assumption that sensible things exist independently of perceiving minds. He did **not** say that objects vanish whenever one individual stops looking. His account also includes an eternal spirit. See [section 6 of *Principles of Human Knowledge*][berkeley].

**Question:** Is access to data enough to call something perception? What would we need to know about the perceiver?

## Merleau-Ponty: perceiving by doing

For Merleau-Ponty, our body is not just a device that delivers information to a detached mind. Perception happens through our involvement with the world and our possibilities for action.

Touch recognition gives this an everyday form: movement helps us recognize edges, materials, weight, and patterns. We may understand something through doing before finding words for it.

**Bridge to creativity:** When you draw, sculpt, play music, edit a video, or design an interaction, you discover possibilities by working with materials. Not every important choice is settled before you begin. How might using AI change that process?

This observation does not settle whether AI systems can have subjective experiences.

For more context see the [publisher's introduction to *Phenomenology of Perception*][merleau-intro] or the [original French preface][merleau-fr].

## Using AI to explore, not to decide

Open the **[Perception NotebookLM][perception-nlm]** when you want context or source leads. It is an *optional* tool, not an additional classroom step.

Questions to try:

> How does Merleau-Ponty connect movement, bodily experience, and understanding? Identify a supporting source.

> Explain the difference between a robot holding a map of a room and a person finding their way through the room. Which parts are technical and which are philosophical?

> Suggest three questions about AI and embodied creativity that could become artistic investigations. For each, identify the human experience at stake.

A NotebookLM or chatbot answer is an interpretation. Check quoted historical claims against the sources it cites.
