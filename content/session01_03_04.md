# Perception Workshop: Thinking Like a Philosopher

What would count as perception in an AI system? Begin with your own examples, try an experiment, and use Berkeley and Merleau-Ponty to reconsider your answers.

**No advance reading required.** Stay with the **same partner throughout**. No switching groups or formal presentations. You will use short resources during the activities; full podcasts and readings are available for later exploration.

## Workshop at a glance

This **85-minute workshop** starts after the break in [Session 01](./session01.md). The timings include short resource checks, pair reflection, and closing.

| Time | Activity | Resource to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 min | Data or Experience? | Your examples, a NotebookLM question, and the shared board |
| 15 min | If the Sensors Go Dark | Berkeley introduction, then one short source passage |
| 20 min | Embodied or Disembodied? | An AI description, your experience, then Merleau-Ponty |
| 20 min | AI as a Philosopher | An LLM and a source you can inspect |
| 20 min | Creative application and closing | A pair sketch, critique together, and a return to opening ideas |

Open the [Maurice vs George Miro board][miro-perception] and the [perception resource guide](./session01_03.md). Keep a small working area for your pair: **initial idea → observation or source → revised idea → open question**. Paper works too. Keep notes brief. Your discussion stays within your pair; the teacher may highlight a few examples, but there are no presentations.

## Activity 1: Data or Experience?

![Data or experience](../assets/data_or_experience.png)

**Goal:** Find out what you mean by perception.

**Duration:** 10 minutes: 5 to choose, 3 with NotebookLM, 2 to compare.

**Format:** Pairs.

### Try it

Consider these examples:

- Recognizing a cat in an image
- Feeling the cat’s fur
- Detecting sadness in a voice recording
- Feeling sad because of someone’s tone
- Navigating a dark room
- A robot mapping a room with lidar

**First (5 min):** For each example, decide whether you associate it mainly with **human perception**, **AI perception**, or **both / uncertain**. Give a short reason. These are initial ideas, not an answer key.

### Ask NotebookLM (3 min)

Only after making your choices, open the [Perception NotebookLM notebook](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/af5ed21e-57cd-4d0a-b2a1-8c96e3e75d74) and ask the [Activity 1 question](./session01_03.md#in-class-a-focused-investigation):

> What is the difference between recognizing a pattern in data and experiencing something? Explain using a cat as an example, in three simple sentences.

If NotebookLM is slow or unavailable, discuss the question without the tool.

### Compare (2 min)

Did NotebookLM introduce a distinction that makes you reconsider one choice? **You do not have to agree with it.** Note one disagreement or change of mind on the board or paper, and keep your initial choices for the closing reflection.

## Activity 2: If the Sensors Go Dark

![A sensor goes dark](../assets/a_sensor_goes_dark.png)

**Goal:** Separate questions about existence from questions about access and representation.

**Duration:** 15 minutes: 4 to respond, 5 with a resource, 6 to reconsider and discuss.

**Format:** Pairs.

### Try it

Imagine a robot with a camera and a stored map of a room. Its camera is disconnected, but its computer keeps running and the map is retained.

**Does its world still exist?**

Write a two-sentence answer. Say what you mean by “its world”: the physical room, its stored representation, its current input, or a possible experience.

Now change one condition: the computer is switched off, but its storage remains intact. Does that change your answer? What does technical reasoning settle, and what does it leave open?

### Bring in Berkeley

Open the [Perception NotebookLM notebook](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/af5ed21e-57cd-4d0a-b2a1-8c96e3e75d74) and use the focused [Activity 2 question](./session01_03.md#in-class-a-focused-investigation). Ask how Berkeley explains what happens when one person stops perceiving something. Check a key claim against [section 6 of his *Principles of Human Knowledge*][berkeley-section-6], rather than reading everything.

Berkeley's account includes perception by an eternal spirit; it is not simply the claim that objects disappear when an individual stops looking.

### Reconsider

Revise your answer, or defend it more precisely. Does a stored map count as perception? What would justify treating a computer as a perceiving mind in Berkeley's sense?

Record one distinction the resource helped you make. Applying Berkeley to AI is our thought experiment, not a claim he made about computers.

## Activity 3: Embodied or Disembodied?

![Embodied or disembodied](../assets/embodied_or_disembodied.png)

**Goal:** Investigate how bodily action shapes perception and how an AI description might influence what you notice.

**Duration:** 20 minutes.

**Format:** Pairs throughout.

### 1. Ask AI to Describe the Experience (3 min)

Ask an LLM:

> Describe how someone might recognize an everyday object using only touch, without seeing it. What sensations and hand movements would help them identify it? Keep your answer under 100 words.

Read the response together. Keep it so you can compare it with your own observations.

### 2. Try Touch Recognition (6 min)

**Use something you already have**, such as a pen, eraser, keyring, or charging cable. One partner chooses a safe object and keeps it out of sight. The other closes their eyes, explores it by touch, and tries to identify it. Then swap roles using a different object. No special materials are needed.

Notice how you move your hand, what you expect to find, and when you feel certain about the object. Choose only objects you are comfortable sharing and touching. If touch recognition is not suitable for you, use the alternative introduced in class.

Before discussing, each write two or three observations in your own words. What did movement reveal that a single touch did not? Was there something you recognized before you could describe it?


### 3. Compare the Accounts (4 min)

Return to the AI description:

- What did it describe well?
- What did you notice that it left out or described differently?
- Did its wording give you something to pay attention to, or influence how you described the experience?

Keep one observation separate from your interpretation. For example, what you actually did with your hand is different from an explanation of why you did it.

### 4. Bring in Merleau-Ponty (3 min)

Use the [short Merleau-Ponty introduction](./session01_03.md#merleau-ponty-the-lived-body) or ask the [Perception NotebookLM notebook](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/af5ed21e-57cd-4d0a-b2a1-8c96e3e75d74) why bodily movement matters for perception. Use the idea to reconsider one observation. You do not need to do both.

What would a robot need to reproduce the behavior you observed? Would reproducing that behavior also establish that it has a lived experience?

### 5. Reflect with Your Partner (4 min)

Compare what **each of you noticed** during the experiment. Discuss:

> Did the AI help us notice something, or lead us to describe the experience in its terms?

Differences between your experiences can raise questions, but they do not prove that the AI description caused them. Together, note one question for the next activity.

## Activity 4: AI as a Philosopher

![AI as a philosopher](../assets/ai_%20as_%20a_philosopher.png)

**Goal:** Investigate how an LLM explains philosophy and how that shapes your understanding.

**Duration:** 20 minutes: 4 to generate, 8 to check, 5 to revise, 3 to discuss.

**Format:** Pairs, with one device per pair or a shared class response. Swap the tool and note-taking roles from Activity 3.

### Ask

Choose a philosopher and ask an LLM:

> Explain how Berkeley's account of perception might help us question the robot whose camera is disconnected. Keep it under 150 words. Distinguish his historical claims from your application to AI.

Or:

> Explain how Merleau-Ponty's account of embodied perception might help us think about a robot picking up a cup. Keep it under 150 words. Distinguish his historical claims from your application to AI.

You can try a philosopher role-play afterward, but treat its output as generated interpretation, not a quotation from the philosopher.

### Check

Choose **one claim** from the response and compare it with a source in the [verification routes](./session01_03.md#verification-routes). Berkeley's section 6 is the default short, English-language route. For Merleau-Ponty, use the specified passage or an excerpt supplied in class.

NotebookLM can help locate and explain a passage. Open the cited source and inspect it: agreement between two AI responses is not verification.

Record:

| LLM claim | Source and passage | Your assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quote or paraphrase one claim | Title and section, page, or paragraph | Supported, oversimplified, contradicted, or not settled by this passage, and why |

Also note something useful in the explanation and something it leaves out. If the source does not address the application to AI, mark that limit rather than treating it as evidence for or against the application.

### Revise and share

Rewrite the claim in your own words using what you found. Take turns explaining your correction or remaining doubt **to your partner**, pointing to the passage that led you to it.

Did the model help you understand an idea, or make one interpretation feel more obvious than it should? What could you establish from the text, and what remains your own argument?

## Creative Application and Closing

**Duration:** 20 minutes.

**Format:** Same pairs, then finish individually.

### Develop an Idea (7 min)

Choose one question from the workshop. Sketch or describe an artwork, interface, or robot that lets someone explore it.

Specify:

- What the participant encounters and does
- What the system senses or responds to
- Which question about learning, perception, or AI interpretation the interaction raises

Use one observation or source from the session to explain a design choice. A rough sketch with a few notes is enough.

### Challenge and Improve Your Idea (6 min)

Stay with your partner. One person explains the sketch while the other imagines being a participant: **What would I do? What might confuse me? What could I learn?** After three minutes, switch roles. Make one small improvement to the sketch.

### Return to the Opening Questions (5 min)

With your partner, revisit one choice from Data or Experience? and the question about learning through failure. Would you answer differently now? Update your note on the board or paper with **one reason** from an experience, argument, or source.

The teacher will briefly highlight a few different answers. No pair needs to present its work.

### Individual Closing Note (2 min)

Write:

- **I first thought…**
- **I now think / still think… because…**
- **I am still unsure about…**

Keep your note with your pair's work. The aim is a more precise question and a better-supported position.
