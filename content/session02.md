# Session 02: Digital Creativity Lab

**Central question:** How can we use generative AI to make intentional creative work, and how can we test whether other people experience it as intended?

Today is a **hands-on studio**. We begin with one shared creative experiment, then you work on your own **Assignment 1: Digital Project on AI and Society** with access to individual help and peer feedback.

**Duration:** 140 minutes, including a 10-minute break. Bring whatever you already have: an idea, sketches, files, unfinished work, or a nearly completed project. **You do not need a finished prototype to participate.**

**Tools:** For the opening experiment we will use an available image generator, potentially [Replicate](https://replicate.com/explore), with an AI writing assistant if useful. The teacher will explain access in class. **For your own project, use the tools that fit your work:** image, text, video, audio, editing, coding, physical making, or a combination. There is **no required studio tool** and no requirement to use Replicate.

**What you keep:** your experiment, useful progress on your own project, comments from a peer, and next steps. These are working materials, **not a separate submission for this session**.

## 1. Opening: Where are you now? (5 min)

**Do (individual):** Identify where you are with your project:

- **Still shaping the idea:** I need to clarify my question, audience, or creative format.
- **Making something:** I have begun producing or testing material.
- **Nearly finished:** I need an outside perspective or help with a particular issue.

Write **one question** you would like help with today. It might be about your concept, technical difficulties, meaning, presentation, or the meaningful non-AI element.

**Produce:** One sentence beginning **"Today I need to..."**. Keep it available if you request a short consultation.

## 2. Creative experiment: The Artist's Statement (20 min)

**Scenario:** An artist has made an artwork using generative AI and wants to exhibit it. The gallery has asked for an image and an artist's statement, but some visitors are skeptical about AI in art.

**Your challenge (pairs):** Invent this artist's image and write a short statement from one of two fictional positions. The artist **did use AI** in both versions.

| Role | Your intention |
| --- | --- |
| **A. Defend AI** | Explain why using AI is a meaningful artistic choice. Make clear where the artist's intention and work lie. |
| **B. Hide AI** | Write as a fictional artist trying to convince the audience the work was made without AI. How does this change the language, story, and claims? |

The contradiction is deliberate. **Role B is fictional persuasion to examine trust**, not advice to misrepresent AI use in your actual academic work. Be accurate about AI use in your real project and reflection.

**Do (pairs):**

1. **Choose a role and artistic direction (3 min).** What is your fictional artwork about, and what should the viewer believe?
2. **Make the image (8 min).** Try an image-generation tool. Change your prompt or approach at least once, then choose one image. If you cannot generate images, use an AI image shown by the teacher and focus on how to frame it.
3. **Write the statement (6 min).** Create a **60–100-word** artist's statement. You may use an LLM to draft it, but revise it yourselves to fit the image and the assigned role.
4. **Check the result (3 min).** Decide what you changed and why. Does the text make the image seem more personal, valuable, or credible?

**Produce:** **One image + one artist's statement (60–100 words) + one sentence** explaining a deliberate change you made. Keep the versions or prompts you used if you want to return to them.

## 3. Discussion: Does the story change the artwork? (10 min)

**Do:** We will look at a few volunteered image-and-text combinations, ideally from both roles. Before the artist's role is revealed, consider:

- What does the statement make you believe about the artwork or the artist?
- Does it affect how much you value or trust the image?
- Which decisions came from your pair, and what did the AI contribute?
- What changes when you learn that a statement was deliberately misleading?

**Produce:** **One observation** about how authorship, language, intention, or transparency affected your interpretation. No formal presentations from every pair.

**Break (10 min)**

## 4. Open Project Studio: Work on your project (55 min)

**This is the main part of the class.** Continue your existing Assignment 1 project. There is no common prototype format, and you do not need to start over because of today's experiment.

**Do (individual):**

1. **Set one concrete goal (first 5 min).** Choose the most useful thing you can realistically work on today. If you need the teacher, prepare a specific question and ask for a short consultation.
2. **Make progress (next 45 min).** Produce, edit, combine, test, or refine your materials. Work in the tools or medium your project requires. The teacher will circulate and prioritize students who are stuck or need help making a decision.
3. **Choose what to show (last 5 min).** Select something a classmate can react to in the peer test: a sketch, short concept, draft image, written passage, sound clip, video segment, interface, or the actual work.

### Different starting points are fine

| If you have... | Work on... | Show your partner... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **An idea but no material yet** | Make the situation, affected people, intended audience experience, and possible form more specific | A short concept description and a sketch, sequence, or references |
| **Work in progress** | Make or refine **one part** that communicates your central question | A draft, excerpt, image, audio clip, interaction, or test |
| **An almost-finished project** | Check clarity, timing, audience experience, and how you explain your own contribution | The work, or the closest version you can share |

Remember the assignment's key ingredients: **one specific AI-and-society question**, **at least one AI-assisted element**, and **a meaningful human-made element without AI**. Your final project may take many forms. Use the **official assignment brief** for all submission requirements and deadlines.

**Produce:** The most useful progress for *your* project, **plus one question you want your peer to help you test**. A complete prototype is **not required**.

**Need inspiration?** Revisit [Human Learning NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/f147adf8-e289-4bc4-938e-350a61e5a484) or [Perception NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/af5ed21e-57cd-4d0a-b2a1-8c96e3e75d74), but don't spend the entire studio generating ideas if you can start making something.

## 5. Peer testing: What does your audience understand? (25 min)

You will each have a chance to **show something** and receive an honest audience response. Testing is useful even when the work is unfinished.

**What you can test:**

- **Only a concept?** Test whether your idea and the people affected are clear and interesting. This is *concept feedback*, not proof that the future artwork communicates.
- **Work in progress?** Test what people actually notice and whether the material creates the intended effect.
- **Nearly finished?** Test the overall experience and any remaining ambiguity.

**Do (pairs):**

1. **Get ready (3 min).** Choose what to show and one thing you want to find out.
2. **First person's test (10 min).** Show the material **without explaining its meaning first**, if possible. If you have only an idea, read your concise concept description. Your partner tells you what they think it is about, what they noticed or felt, and what remains unclear. **Then** explain your intention and discuss the difference.
3. **Swap roles (10 min).** Repeat for the other person.
4. **Record the insight (2 min).** Each of you notes the most useful feedback.

**Feedback prompts for the viewer:**

> "I think this is about..."  
> "What struck me or confused me was..."  
> "One question or suggestion I have is..."

**Produce (individual):** **Three lines**:

- **I wanted to communicate...**
- **My partner understood or experienced...**
- **The change I will make is...**

The aim is to find out how someone else receives the work, **not** to give it a grade.

## 6. Improve and decide next steps (15 min)

**Do (individual):**

1. **Work for 10 min** on one change suggested by the peer test, or make a concrete plan if the change needs more time.
2. **Use the final 5 min** to identify what remains before the deadline and ask a last practical question if needed.

**Produce:** **One improvement, or a clear next action**, and a short record of what you tried, what feedback you received, and what you changed. This may also be useful when writing your later reflection.
