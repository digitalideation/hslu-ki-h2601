# Session 02: Digital Creativity Lab

<img src="../assets/session02_01.png" alt="Session 02 illustration" width="640">

**Central question:** How can we use generative AI to make intentional creative work, and how can we find out what an audience actually understands?

**140 minutes, including a 10-minute break · Shared experiment, individual studio, and peer testing**

Bring **what you already have** for Assignment 1: an idea, sketches, files, work in progress, or a nearly finished project. You do **not** need to build a new prototype or use a particular tool. Keep short notes on what you try and the feedback you receive. Nothing from this workshop is a separate graded submission.

## 1. Experiment: The Artist's Statement (35 min)

### Where is your project now? (5 min)

**Do (individual):** Decide what you most need today: **clarify an idea**, **make or refine something**, or **test nearly finished work**. Think of one question for an individual consultation if you'd like help.

**Produce:** **One line:** "Today I need to..."

### The Artist's Statement (20 min)

**Scenario:** A fictional artist has made a work **using AI** and is submitting it to an exhibition. Some visitors distrust AI-assisted art. Your pair must create an image and an artist's statement.

Choose one role:

| Role | What your fictional artist wants |
| --- | --- |
| **A. Defend AI** | Convince visitors that using AI is a legitimate artistic decision; explain the artist's contribution. |
| **B. Conceal AI** | Convince visitors the work was made without AI, even though it wasn't. Explore how an artist's story can manipulate trust. |

**Do (pairs):**

1. **Choose a role (3 min):** Decide what your artwork is about and what its audience should believe.
2. **Generate an image (8 min):** Use an available image tool. Change your prompt or approach at least once. If generation is unavailable, use an example provided by the lecturer.
3. **Write the statement (6 min):** Produce **60–100 words** explaining the fictional artist's work. You may start with AI-generated writing, but revise it yourselves.
4. **Review (3 min):** Does your image and statement communicate your chosen position? Identify one deliberate change you made.

**Produce:** **One image, one 60–100-word artist statement, and one sentence** explaining your revision.

*Role B is a fictional exercise about credibility and deception. Do not misrepresent AI use in your actual assessed work.*

### Discussion: Does the story change the artwork? (10 min)

**Do:** View a few volunteered image-and-statement pairs. Before hearing the assigned role, consider: Does the text change the meaning or value of the image? What decisions did the students make? What changes when a claim of authorship turns out to be misleading?

**Produce:** **One observation** about intention, trust, or creative contribution, ready to share if invited.

**Break (10 min)**

## 2. Open Project Studio (55 min)

**This is time for your existing Assignment 1 project.** The lecturer will offer short individual consultations, prioritizing people who are blocked or need help making a decision. You don't have to start over or use the tool from the opening experiment.

**Do (individual):**

1. **First 5 min:** Choose one useful, achievable goal. If you need the lecturer, prepare **one specific question** about your concept, technique, creative choices, or presentation.
2. **Next 45 min:** Work on your project. Make, edit, combine, test, or refine material using your preferred tools.
3. **Last 5 min:** Select something your partner can react to during peer testing.

| Where you are | What to work on | What to show later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Only an idea** | Define the AI-related question, affected people, audience experience, and possible form | A short description plus a sketch, sequence, or references |
| **Making the project** | Improve one meaningful part | A draft image, text, sound, video segment, interface, or experiment |
| **Nearly finished** | Check clarity, the viewing experience, and what remains to fix | The work or its closest shareable version |

**Produce:** **Useful progress on your project and one question for your peer.** A completed prototype is **not required**.

The official brief sets the assignment requirements. Keep its distinction between **AI-assisted work** and **a meaningful contribution made without AI** in mind.

If you're still looking for a question, you can use the [Human Learning NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/f147adf8-e289-4bc4-938e-350a61e5a484) or [Perception NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/af5ed21e-57cd-4d0a-b2a1-8c96e3e75d74) from Session 01.

## 3. Peer testing (25 min)

**Goal:** Discover what another person understands or experiences **before** you explain your intention. If you only have an idea, you're testing the **clarity of the concept**, not yet the artwork itself.

**Do (pairs):**

1. **Prepare (3 min):** Pick what to show and what you want to find out.
2. **First person (10 min):** Show your material, ideally without explaining its meaning. Your partner responds first; then compare their interpretation with your intention. If you only have a concept, briefly describe it instead.
3. **Swap (10 min):** Repeat for the other person.
4. **Record (2 min):** Note the most useful difference between what you intended and what your partner understood.

**The viewer can begin with:**

> I think this is about...  
> What interested or confused me was...  
> One question I still have is...

**Produce:** **Three short lines:**

- **I wanted to communicate...**
- **My partner understood or experienced...**
- **One change I could make is...**

This isn't a grade or a formal class presentation.

## 4. Improve and plan (15 min)

**Do (individual):** Spend **10 minutes** implementing one useful improvement or planning a change that needs more time. Use the final **5 minutes** to identify the next thing to do before submitting.

**Produce:** **One improvement or concrete next action**, plus a brief note on what you tested, what feedback you received, and how you responded. It may help with your later reflection.

The official Assignment 1 brief specifies **29 October 2026** for the digital project and **15 November 2026** for the reflection. Follow the module's submission instructions.
