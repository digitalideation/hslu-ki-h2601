# Part 2: Human Learning - The Failed Animal

**Guiding question: Does failure actually help us learn?**

You don't need to read or listen to everything. Use [NotebookLM][notebooklm-notebook] to explore Charles Pépin's ideas and see whether you agree with him.

## In class: A focused investigation (20 min)

Work with the **same partner as in Part 1**.

1. **Remember an experience (3 min).** Think back to the example you shared at the start of class. Did making a mistake help you learn something? Or did the learning come afterward?
2. **Ask NotebookLM (9 min).** Open the [Human Learning notebook][notebooklm-notebook] and ask:

   > Why does Charles Pépin think failure can be useful for learning? Explain simply and give one example from the sources.

   Read the answer together. If NotebookLM points to a passage, take a quick look. If you cannot access it, use the [short English transcript section on learning through culture and failure](../slides/static/vids/philo_en.md#learning-through-culture-and-failure).
3. **Talk with your partner (6 min).** Does Pépin's explanation fit what happened to you? **Is failure always useful, or can it sometimes teach us nothing?**
4. **Wrap up (2 min).** We'll finish with a short question from the teacher. No presentations or reports are needed.

## Key ideas to explore

These are ideas raised by Pépin's philosophical argument, not conclusions you must agree with.

### Failure and learning

Pépin calls humans "denatured animals" and contrasts a foal's early ability to walk with the lengthy process of a child learning to walk. His point is that human weakness and repeated attempts may create possibilities for learning. Does that comparison convince you?

### Culture and other people

Pépin suggests that our dependence on learning leads us to rely on culture: family, education, advice, books, tutorials, and relationships. Think about how other people contributed to what you learned from your own mistake.

### Freedom and invention

The argument extends beyond getting better at a task: because humans are not guided by fixed instincts alone, Pépin connects learning to the possibility of inventing ourselves. He links this to the philosophical idea that "existence precedes essence." Where might you agree or disagree?

## Further exploration

The following are optional questions for later discussion, an individual project, or a deeper investigation.

- **The foal and the baby:** What does the contrast reveal about instinct, learning, and what we mean by success? The transcript's numerical examples are part of the argument; they are not independently established here.
- **The virtues of failure:** When has failing led to an unexpected discovery or changed your direction?
- **Learning from others:** Which parts of learning can come from information, and which depend on relationships and social context?
- **Freedom and invention:** Does having to learn rather than acting instinctively make us more creative or free? What would challenge Pépin's view?
