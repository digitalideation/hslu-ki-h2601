# Lecturer Notes: Session 02 — Digital Creativity Lab

[Student-facing Session 02](./session02.md)

## Purpose and priorities

Students have approximately three days before the **29 October 2026** Assignment 1 project deadline (assuming this class is held on 26 October). The priority is **making progress, individual help, and useful peer testing**, not teaching every AI tool.

**140 min including 10 min break:** 5 opening; 20 shared creative experiment; 10 plenary; 10 break; 55 individual studio; 25 peer testing; 15 improvement.

The session should accommodate students with only an idea, work in progress, or almost finished projects. **No uniform prototype is required**. Keep the official assessment brief, not this workshop, as the authority on deliverables and submission.

## Tools: Replicate and alternatives

**Recommendation:** Keep Replicate as a **lecturer-curated option for the common experiment**, not a mandatory platform for all students. Choose a working model in advance, demonstrate one generation, and make a fallback image available. If you permit student use, explain exactly how access is granted before class, check usage costs and account permissions, and set a spending policy. **Never display, paste, distribute, or commit your personal Replicate API token.** Do not encourage students to share a personal login.

The open studio is **tool-agnostic**. Students may use tools they already know and that fit their project medium. Avoid new account creation where the burden exceeds the value. The fictional "hide AI" role in the experiment is a critical examination of persuasion; remind students to disclose actual AI use accurately in their assessed projects.

Consider checking provider terms, model permissions, quotas, billed generation, and acceptable uploaded material. Avoid entering private student data into shared third-party accounts. An image output generated or supplied by the lecturer is an acceptable fallback.

## Preparing the experiment (20 min)

- Choose one accessible image model and a low-friction generation path. Test it in advance. Avoid long generation queues, payment prompts, and restrictions that students cannot resolve.
- Have **one or two image outputs ready** for students whose device/account cannot access generation.
- Explain the fictional gallery situation and distribute A (defend AI) or B (conceal AI) to pairs. Students can choose or be assigned, but seek a mixture of positions.
- Give them the target output: **one image, 60–100-word artist statement, one explanation of a deliberate revision**.
- The image is not necessarily impressive: the intended challenge is the relationship between artwork, its narrative, and audience trust.
- Invite 2–3 voluntary pairs in the ten-minute plenary. Ask about the different effects of statements, the creative choices students made, and the ethics of misrepresentation.

## Open Project Studio: making individual help work with ~30 students (55 min)

**The consultation objective is meaningful decisions, not talking to everyone.** A feasible pattern:

1. **First 5 min:** everyone writes their goal and optionally requests help with one specific question. Use a show of hands, a small paper queue, or an agreed class board. Prioritize blocked students and students without workable ideas.
2. **Next ~45 min:** hold **short ~5-minute individual consultations** while students work. At most about **8 consultations** fit comfortably, allowing for movement and overruns. Do not promise to see all 30. If multiple students have the same technical issue, give one brief group answer.
3. **Last 5 min:** have students pick the material or concept they will show to a peer. Help them prepare a viewable item, not a polished artifact.

**Conversation starters:** What is your specific AI-and-society question? Who is affected? What should your audience experience? What is your meaningful non-AI element? What's the smallest thing you could finish before submission? What do you want help deciding right now?

**For students without an idea:** Aim for a focused question, relevant stakeholder, feasible format, and immediate first test, not endless brainstorming. NotebookLM materials from Session 01 remain available.

**For nearly finished students:** Offer specific feedback on clarity, documentation, disclosure, and what the audience actually experiences.

## Peer testing (25 min)

- 3 min preparation; 10 min first creator; 10 min second creator; 2 min note-taking.
- The viewer first reacts without being given the intended meaning, *if actual material exists*. For concept-only students, let them give a succinct explanation and explicitly call it a concept test.
- Invite the viewer to begin with **"I think this is about..."**, **"What struck me..."**, **"I still wonder..."**.
- The creator notes a mismatch between intention and interpretation and decides one change.
- Do not make students present to the full class; two-way pair exchange suffices.

## Final 15 minutes

Ask for one practical change during the first 10 minutes and one realistic next action in the final 5. Students should keep the learning for their later reflection but are not submitting additional materials to the lecturer.

## Risks and contingencies

- **Tool failures:** model queues, login issues, cost concerns. Use saved outputs and edit statements instead.
- **Time pressure:** resist extending the 20-minute shared activity; give priority to 55-minute studio and 25-minute peer testing.
- **Sensitive or private material:** don't upload unpublished peer material, private data, or third-party images to a shared model without permission.
- **Status differences:** students near completion should not be made to restart; students at an early stage should not be expected to show a finished prototype.
- **Official deadlines:** confirm with the module coordinator if any details differ from the supplied brief.
