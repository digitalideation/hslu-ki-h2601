# I.BA_PHKI.H2601

## About this course

What does it mean to learn, perceive, and create? How do these processes differ between humans and artificial intelligence, and what can experimenting with AI reveal about our own ways of thinking?

We explore these questions through short technical introductions, philosophy, discussion, and hands-on experiments. The aim is not to arrive at a definitive answer to whether AI "really" learns or perceives, but to examine the assumptions behind those claims and connect them to creative practice.

**How to use these materials:** The podcasts, NotebookLM notebooks, readings, and Miro boards are a resource library, **not a checklist to complete**. In class, we begin with questions and activities, then consult selected excerpts when they help us investigate an idea. Full materials are available for independent exploration. **No advance reading is required for Session 01.**

## Session 01: Learning, Failure, and Perception

![Session 01 image](./assets/session01_01.png)

**Central question:** When we say that humans and AI "learn" or "perceive," are we talking about the same thing?

### Guiding questions

- How do AI systems learn from examples and errors, and how does that compare with human learning?
- Can failure be productive rather than merely something to minimize?
- Is recognizing patterns the same as perceiving or experiencing the world?
- How do our bodies, contexts, and interactions shape what we know?

### Session schedule

The session lasts **140 minutes, including a 10-minute break**. Times are minutes from the start.

| Time | Activity | How we work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Opening: learning through failure | Reflect individually, then discuss in pairs |
| 5–25 | Part 1: AI Learning | Short introduction, pair investigation, class discussion |
| 25–45 | Part 2: Human Learning | Groups of four, short Pépin passage, contrasting positions |
| 45–55 | **Break** | |
| 55–65 | Data or Experience? | Pairs, then discuss a disagreement |
| 65–80 | If the Sensors Go Dark | Groups of four, using Berkeley |
| 80–100 | Embodied or Disembodied? | Experiment in pairs, compare an AI account with experience |
| 100–120 | AI as a Philosopher | Check an LLM claim against a source, then compare findings |
| 120–140 | Creative application and closing | Design in groups, exchange ideas, reflect individually |

Keep the same partner through the session. When needed, two pairs join into a group of four. In AI activities, share a device and alternate between operating the tool and recording observations or checking sources. Everyone will explain something to peers, but there are no formal slide presentations. We will hear a few contrasting examples in whole-class discussions.

### Before the break: Learning

**Opening (5 min):** Think of something you learned through difficulty, perhaps in a creative project or an everyday task. What changed: knowledge, behavior, confidence, or understanding? Note your example, then compare it with your partner. Share only what you are comfortable discussing.

**Part 1: AI Learning (20 min)**

**When an AI system "learns," what changes?** After the short introduction, examine the machine-learning example introduced in class with your partner.

- What information does the system receive?
- How is an error or a successful result identified?
- What changes through training, and how could we check whether it learned something useful?

Write one sentence explaining learning in this example. Compare it with your opening example. What seems similar, and where does the comparison become difficult?

**Part 2: Human Learning (20 min)**

**What do we learn through failure, and what makes a failure meaningful to us?** Join another pair and choose one example to discuss.

Read the short passage in the [English Pépin transcript](./slides/static/vids/philo_en.md) beginning "Well, because we don't know how to do things instinctively" and ending "So there are virtues in failure." Alternatively, listen to the excerpt played in class.

What does Pépin's argument help explain, and where would you challenge it? Compare your example with machine learning. What roles do other people, emotions, and purposes play?

Record **one claim you can defend** and **one open question**. The full [Human Learning resources](./content/session01_02.md) are there if you want to investigate further.

### After the break: Perception and Practice

**What would count as perception in an AI system, and how might AI influence our own interpretations?**

Follow the **[Perception workshop](./content/session01_03_04.md)** for the final **85 minutes**, including the creative application and reflection. Its four activities use your own examples, a Berkeley thought experiment, a bodily experiment, and an AI-generated philosophical explanation. The [Perception resource guide](./content/session01_03.md) provides short introductions and passages when the activities call for them.

In the embodied activity, compare an AI description with what you actually notice. In the philosophical activity, check a claim against a source. At the end, turn one question into an artwork, interface, or interaction idea and share it with another group.

By the end of the session, you should be able to explain one useful distinction, support it with an observation or source, and identify a question worth exploring through creative practice.
